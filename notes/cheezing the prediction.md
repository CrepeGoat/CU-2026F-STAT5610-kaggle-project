# calculating true counts from reported F-scores

> suppose for any set of predictions, we can calculate a given F-score. can we use this to find the true response values?

suppose we predict all positives -> $\text{TN} = 0, \text{TP} = \text{P}$

-> using the [[#F-score via true pos. & true neg.|true pos. & neg. form]]:

$$
\begin{align}
F _{1}
	&= \frac{2 \cancelto{\text{P}}{\text{TP}}}{\cancelto{\text{P}}{\text{TP}} + n \cancel{- \text{TN}}} \\
	&= \frac{2 \text{P}}{\text{P} + n} \\
	&= \frac{2}{1 + n / \text{P}} \\
\to \frac{n}{\text{P}}
	&= \frac{2}{F _{1}} - 1 \\
	&= \frac{2 - F _{1}}{F _{1}} \\
\to \text{P} = \text{TP} + \text{FN}
	&= \frac{n F _{1}}{2 - F _{1}} \\
\end{align}
$$

given this, we revisit the F-score equation for the general case with arbitrary predictions (using [[#F-score via true pos. & pred. pos.|the pos. & pred. pos. form]]):

$$
\begin{align}
F _{1}
	&= \frac{2 \text{TP}}{\text{PP} + \text{P}} \\
\to \text{TP}
	&= \frac{1}{2} F _{1} (\text{PP} + \text{P}) \\
\end{align}
$$

where $\text{PP}$ is the number of *predicted positives*, which we also know.

=> this means **every future guess after the first can be used to count the number of positives in our predictions**

# calculating true counts amidst error

Kaggle intentionally truncates decimal points, so as to avoid "probing". Truncation means our F-score is now only accurate up to some error $F _{1} \pm \varepsilon$:

$$
\begin{align}
\to \tilde{\text{P}}
	&= \frac{n (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon)}{2 - F _{1} \mp \varepsilon} \\
\to \text{TP}
	&= \frac{1}{2} (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon) (\text{PP} + \tilde{\text{P}}) \\
\end{align}
$$

the derivative of the positives w.r.t. the F-score can be written as:

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{d F _{1}} \text{P}
	&= \frac{d}{d F _{1}} \left(\frac{n F _{1}}{2 - F _{1}}\right) \\
	&= \frac{n (2 \cancel{- F _{1}}) \cancel{- (- n F _{1})}}{(2 - F _{1})^{2}} \\
	&= \frac{2 n}{n^{2} (2 - F _{1})^{2}} \\
	&> 0 \\
\end{align}
$$

the derivative of the *true* positives w.r.t. the F-score can be thus written as:

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{d F _{1}} \text{TP}
	&= \frac{d}{d F _{1}} \left(\frac{1}{2} F _{1}(\text{PP} + \text{P})\right) \\
	&= \frac{1}{2} \left((\text{PP} + \text{P}) + F _{1} \cdot \frac{d}{d F _{1}} (\text{PP} + \text{P})\right) \\
	&= \frac{1}{2} \left((\text{PP} + \text{P}) + F _{1} \cdot \frac{d}{d F _{1}}\text{P}\right) \\
	&> 0 \\
\end{align}
$$


-> the derivatives are positive over all F-scores $F _{1} \in [0, 1]$

-> the bounds for each of the positives and the true positives can simply be computed using the upper and lower bounds of the F-score itself

let $a _{+}, a _{-}$ denote the upper- and lower-bounds of some variable $a$

then we can write the bounds as:

$$
\begin{align}
\to \text{P} _{\pm}
	&= \frac{n (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon)}{2 - (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon)} \\
	&= \frac{n (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon)}{2 - F _{1} \mp \varepsilon} \\
\\
\to \underline{\text{TP}}
	&= \frac{1}{2} (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon) (\text{PP} + \text{P} _{\pm}) \\
	&= \frac{1}{2} (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon) 
		\left(\text{PP} + \left(\frac{n (F _{1} \pm \varepsilon)}{2 - F _{1} \mp \varepsilon}\right)\right)
		\\
\end{align}
$$

these bounds should contain the true, integer counts we're looking for

# using total count + half-score rule

given a total count of all positive responses $\text{P}$, we can follow the following process to make predictions:

- fit a model to the data
- predict positive response probabilities for all test data points
- take the $\text{P}$-highest-probability predictions, and predict positively
- take the $(n - \text{P})$-lowest-probability predictions, and predict negatively
	- note that this suggests an implicit threshold at the boundary probability between positive and negative responses
- -> submit, and get the resulting F-score

from [[optimizing the prediction#implication - half-score rule|the half-score rule]], we know that an optimal threshold should be half the corresponding optimal F-score:

this rule depends on two assumptions:
- the threshold / F-score is optimal - this is automatically guaranteed, since we know the number of positive responses
- ⭐️ **the positive response probabilities are correct** - these are generated by our model

=> we can compare our F-score to the effective threshold:

- if they match s.t. $T _{p} \approx \frac{1}{2} F _{1}$, our model is generating appropriate probabilities
- **if they don't match, then our model is not performing correctly**

# e.g. calculations

## e.g. total positive count

given:
- $\hat{y} _{i} = 1$ for all $i \in \lbrace 1, \dots, n\rbrace$
- $n = 10^{5} = 100,000$
- $\lfloor F _{1} \rfloor = 0.04862$

->:

$$
\begin{align}
\to \text{P}
	&= \frac{(10^{5}) (0.04862)}{2 - (0.04862)} \\
	&\approx 2491.57 \\
\end{align}
$$

this value is truncated to a smaller number of digits -> the errors are thus $\pm 10^{-6}$:

$$
\begin{align}
\to \text{P} _{-} = \text{P}
	&\approx 2491.57 \\
\to \text{P} _{+}
	&= \frac{(10^{5}) (0.04863)}{2 - (0.04863)} \\
	&\approx 2492.09 \\
\end{align}
$$

the only integer contained in this bound is 2492:

$$
\therefore \text{P} = 2492
$$
